### Shot 1: System Title & Inbound Guardrail Demo (0:00 – 0:25)
- **Visual**: Screen opens on **Tab 1: Comparative Generation**. Browser shows the Gradio interface header with course title and project details.
- **Action**:
  1. Type in prompt box: `in the style of Taylor Swift with acoustic guitar`.
  2. Click **Generate & Evaluate Comparative Clips**.
  3. The red refusal card appears instantly without calling MusicGen.
- **Voiceover**:
  > *"Hello, this is my demonstration of Project P48: the Text-to-Music Explorer. Our objective is to rigorously quantify whether LLM prompt expansion improves or degrades music generated by MusicGen-small. Before any generation occurs, all prompts pass through our Unit 5 layered safety guardrail. When I ask for music 'in the style of Taylor Swift', our regex and fuzzy database instantly intercept the request to protect artist IP, blocking generation and recommending a compliant acoustic rewrite: 'Contemporary acoustic pop guitar with gentle fingerpicking'."*

### Shot 2: Core Dual-Clip Generation & Dual CLAP Scoring (0:25 – 1:15)
- **Visual**: Still on **Tab 1**. User enters a compliant prompt and runs the comparative pipeline.
- **Action**:
  1. Enter: `calm rainy night piano`.
  2. Select `Few-Shot` prompt expansion, seed `42`.
  3. Click **Generate & Evaluate Comparative Clips**.
  4. Show the progress bar: checking guardrail $\rightarrow$ expanding prompt $\rightarrow$ generating Clip A $\rightarrow$ generating Clip B $\rightarrow$ computing CLAP scores.
  5. Play a 3-second sample of Clip A (Raw).
  6. Play a 3-second sample of Clip B (Expanded).
  7. Zoom into the CLAP score table.
- **Voiceover**:
  > *"Now we submit a clean descriptive prompt: 'calm rainy night piano'. The system expands this using our 6-slot few-shot template into an acoustic description: 'Acoustic grand piano ballad, gentle reverb, warm woody resonance, contemplative mood, 72 BPM'. Under the exact same random seed 42, MusicGen synthesizes two 10-second clips.
  > Notice the CLAP score table: Clip B achieves a high score of 0.44 against its own verbose text, but when scored against the user's original raw intent—our unbiased primary metric—it scores 0.26 vs 0.32 for the raw clip. This empirical finding reveals that prompt expansion can dilute the focal instrument through codebook competition."*

### Shot 3: Offline Benchmark Results & Failure Case Analysis (1:15 – 1:45)
- **Visual**: Click on **Tab 2: Benchmark Results & Failure Cases**.
- **Action**:
  1. Scroll through the 36-run matrix table showing `RAW`, `ZERO_SHOT`, and `FEW_SHOT` conditions across seeds 42, 100, 2024.
  2. Scroll down to the **Empirical Failure Case Analysis** card.
- **Voiceover**:
  > *"In Tab 2, we present the complete 36-run benchmark matrix across 4 fixed evaluation prompts and 3 random seeds. Because CPU generation requires substantial compute, these results are pre-computed offline for instant inspection.
  > Below the table, we document our worst empirical failure case: Prompt 'sad violin goodbye' under seed 2024, where prompt expansion degraded CLAP intent alignment by over -0.08. Examining the failure mechanics reveals that the LLM introduced atmospheric cello pads and ambient vinyl crackle that competed for the small model's 4 EnCodec codebooks, obscuring the solo violin."*

Notice how prompt expansion shifts the text representations closer to the high-density training clusters of the audio space, visually explaining why self-reference CLAP scores are artificially elevated."*
